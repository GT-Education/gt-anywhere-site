## 1. Question

How does paid social creative hand off to a landing page — message match, visual continuity, and the expectations the ad format itself sets on the first screen? What do the Instagram, Facebook and TikTok in-app browsers actually do to a page (viewport height, missing chrome, link behaviour, what breaks), with measured or documented values? What do Meta and TikTok require of a landing page, and what quality signals do they score it on? How does page speed bear on ad cost and delivery? What must the page carry so UTMs and attribution survive three hosts? How do remarketing audiences and Consent Mode v2 constrain design? What carries a HubSpot email visitor into the landing? How does an organic Instagram profile link behave? And what does brand search for "GT School" look like?

## 2. Findings

### 2a. The handoff: message match and visual continuity

| # | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The strongest *measured* evidence for visual continuity is an A/B test, not a vendor claim: experiment **#4854**, 2016-07-01 to 2016-08-29, 239,607 participants (9,488 required), control a Facebook ad showing a person studying, treatment the same ad carrying the **Sistine Chapel image pulled from the landing page**. Conversion rose from **0.49% to 0.73%, a 47.6% relative lift at 100.0% confidence**. The variable changed was the *image shared with the page*, not the copy | NextAfter Research Library, *How congruence between ad and landing page affects conversion*, experiment #4854, https://www.nextafter.com/experiments/how-congruence-between-ad-and-landing-page-affects-conversion/ |
| F2 | The mechanism has a name and a literature: **information scent**, "the user's imperfect estimate of the value that the source will deliver to the user, derived from a representation of the source", "a central concept in the information-foraging theory". When a landing page supplies too little context, users decide "that the page is not worth exploring any longer and simply leave", wasting the positive scent that brought them | Raluca Budiu, Nielsen Norman Group, *Information Scent*, 2 Feb 2020, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/information-scent/ |
| F3 | Attention is concentrated at the top but not confined to it: across 130,000+ eye fixations from 120 participants on a 1920×1080 screen, "users spent about **57%** of their page-viewing time above the fold" and "**74%** of the viewing time was spent in the first two screenfuls, up to 2160px" | Therese Fessenden, Nielsen Norman Group, *Scrolling and Attention*, 15 Apr 2018, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/ |
| F4 | Google's own landing-page guidance says the same thing in operational terms: "Choose a landing page that closely matches your ad and keywords"; "Your landing page should also mirror the call-to-action in your ad text"; "Put important information towards the top of the page. | F5 | TikTok makes ad-to-page consistency an **approval condition**, not a best practice: "Ensure that your ad creative and display name are consistent with your landing page. ### 2c. In-app browsers: what is documented, what is measurable, what is not

| # | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F13 | Neither Meta nor TikTok publishes a viewport height, a chrome height, or a webview capability matrix. Meta's own help article on the in-app browser returns a title and no body to a text fetch (the same failure `R3` recorded as S13 on 2026-09-10, still true on 2026-09-11). **Every in-app height in this file is therefore derived or measured by us, never vendor-documented** | See section 6; corroborated by `research/R3-conversion-and-marketing.md` S13 |
| F14 | The CSS viewport units that exist for this problem are defined against *retractable* UA chrome: the **large viewport** (`lvh`) is "the viewport sized assuming any UA interfaces that are dynamically expanded and retracted to be **retracted**"; the **small viewport** (`svh`) assumes them "to be **expanded**"; the **dynamic viewport** (`dvh`) equals the small viewport when toolbars are expanded and the large viewport when they retract. The failure mode is named: "elements sized to be `100vh` tall will bleed out of the viewport" | Bramus Van Damme, web.dev, *The large, small, and dynamic viewport units*, last updated 2022-11-29, https://web.dev/blog/viewport-units |
| F15 | In-app webviews do not honour that model. "In WebViews used by in-app browsers like Instagram or Facebook, **toolbar behavior is non-standard and viewport unit calculations can be unpredictable**. Always test on actual devices in the specific embedded contexts your users encounter." The article also reports the observed symptom class: "Hero sections overflow by 56–80px on initial load" | OpenReplay Team, *When 100vh Lies: Fixing Mobile Viewport Issues*, 5 Mar 2026, https://blog.openreplay.com/fix-100vh-mobile-viewport/ |
| F16 | The symptom has been reported against Instagram's webview since at least 2020: "the in-app browser in instagram breaks the height of the webpage, which leads to a cut menubar at the bottom" — reported with no measurements, which is the state of the public evidence generally | GitHub issue, cscheffauer/about #6, opened 10 Feb 2020, https://github.com/cscheffauer/about/issues/6 |
| F17 | In-app browsers strip capability, not just height: they lack "bookmarking, UI controls, settings, extensions, and downloads", and bring "slower loading times and compatibility issues". On Android the problem is structural: "Since Apple doesn't allow apps, even browsers, to use their own rendering engine only Android has the problem of bundling in a custom in-app browser instead of using the system WebView" | Paul Esch-Laurent, Master.dev Blog, *The Pitfalls of In-App Browsers*, 17 Jul 2024, https://blog.master.dev/the-pitfalls-of-in-app-browsers/ |
| F18 | Link behaviour differs by app, and it is documented by measurement rather than by the vendors. Krause tested 23 apps on iOS. **TikTok**: "Default browser option: ⛔ No", "User can open in system browser: No button available"; it "subscribes to every keystroke (text inputs) happening on third party websites" and monitors "every tap". **Instagram** and **Facebook**: "Default browser option: ✅ Yes", user can open in system browser; both inject `pcm.js` and subscribe "to every tap on any button, link, image or other component" | Felix Krause, *iOS Privacy: Announcing InAppBrowser.com*, 18 Aug 2022, https://krausefx.com/blog/announcing-inappbrowsercom-see-what-javascript-commands-get-executed-in-an-in-app-browser |
| F19 | Corroborated independently: "Instagram can even detect all text selections on websites" through their in-app browser | Filipe Espósito, 9to5Mac, *Tool shows JavaScript code injected via in-app browser*, 19 Aug 2022, https://9to5mac.com/2022/08/19/tool-shows-injected-javascript-in-app-browser/ |
| F20 | Android's **Custom Tabs** are the well-behaved case and are explicitly *not* what Meta and TikTok use: they give a "Shared cookie jar and permissions model so users don't have to sign-in to sites they are already connected to", and the user's "browsing session, saved passwords, payment methods, and addresses will all show up just like they are accustomed to already" | Chrome for Developers, *Overview of Android Custom Tabs*, last updated 2020-02-04, https://developer.chrome.com/docs/android/custom-tabs |
| F20a | Custom Tabs are also faster by construction — "Pre-warming of the Browser in the background, while avoiding stealing resources from the application" and the ability to "Speed up the page load time by speculatively loading URLs in advance" — none of which a bundled webview does. The performance gap between a Custom Tab and a Meta or TikTok webview is therefore structural, not incidental | Chrome for Developers, *Overview of Android Custom Tabs*, last updated 2020-02-04, https://developer.chrome.com/docs/android/custom-tabs |
| F21 | A bundled Android WebView starts from a different default than Chrome: "Most web browsers on Android—including Chrome—set the viewport to a large size by default. This *wide viewport mode* is about 980 px wide… When your page is rendered in a `WebView`, it doesn't use wide viewport mode by default" | Android Developers, *Support different screens in web apps*, https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/layout/webapps/targeting |
| F21a | Android scales web content by the page's declared target density, and reports the factor to JavaScript: "`WebView` supports `window.devicePixelRatio`… If the value of `window.devicePixelRatio` is 1.0, then the device is considered a medium-density device, and no scaling is applied by default. If the value is 1.5, then the device is considered a high-density device, and the page is scaled 1.5x by default." A page without `width=device-width` is therefore laid out at a width the design never chose | Android Developers, *Support different screens in web apps*, https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/layout/webapps/targeting |
| F22 | `viewport-fit=cover` is the only supported way to reach the physical display edges, and it is paired with an obligation: "It's highly recommended to use the safe area inset variables to ensure that important content doesn't end up outside the display." MDN also warns: "Disabling zooming capabilities by setting `user-scalable` to a value of `no` prevents people experiencing low vision conditions from being able to read and understand page content" | MDN Web Docs, *`<meta name="viewport">`*, https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/meta/name/viewport |

## 3. Principles the studio adopts

| # | Principle | Traces to |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Continuity is carried by the image and the noun, not by the headline.** The one measured congruence experiment moved the *image*; the page's fixed sentence can stay fixed while the ad is briefed from it | F1, F2 |
| P2 | **Design the first screen for the smallest chrome-constrained viewport, not for the device.** The device is 844 tall; the page gets 664. In an in-app browser it gets less, and never gets it back on scroll | F14, F15, F24 |
| P3 | **Never size the first screen with `100vh`.** Use `100svh` with a content-height fallback; treat `dvh` expansion as a bonus that in-app browsers will not deliver | F14, F15 |
| P4 | **Assume no browser chrome.** No back button, no address bar, no reload, no downloads, no reliable "open in browser" (TikTok has none). Every route out of the page must be an element on the page | F17, F18, F20 |
| P5 | **The URL is the attribution carrier; cookies are the fallback.** `_gl`, `fbclid`, `ttclid`, `gclid`, `_hsenc` and `utm_*` must survive every hop, because partitioned and seven-day-capped cookies will not | F30, F33, F35, F37, F38 |
| P6 | **Strip identifiers from reports, never from the URL before the tags read them.** `_fbc` is *manufactured* from `fbclid` on arrival; strip it early and the Meta conversion is unattributable and the 48-hour dedup window never opens | F33, F34 |
| P7 | **Tag conventions are only conventions if GA4's rules accept them.** A medium the rules do not list works only when the source is on Google's 819-row list; where it is not, the session becomes Unassigned | F31, F32 |
| P8 | **Speed is a marketing lever at a scale the design controls.** A 0.1 s change moved lead-gen form progression 21.6% in an audited 30-million-session study; the page is dependency-free and can simply be fast | F26, F28 |
| P9 | **Both platforms review the destination, so the page is part of the ad.** A page that is slow, zoom-dependent, or asks for personal information before it shows content is a rejection or a demotion, not a conversion problem | F6, F7, F8, F12 |
| P10 | **Retargeting is a volume question before it is a design question.** 100 users in 30 days for Display and YouTube; 1,000 for Search. Door-click audiences are worth instrumenting and are not worth designing around until they fill | F39, F40 |

## 4. Anti-patterns

| # | Anti-pattern | Why it fails | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | A full-bleed hero at `height: 100vh` | Bleeds out of the viewport in Safari and unpredictably in webviews; the CTA lands under the app's bottom toolbar, which is exactly the reported Instagram symptom | F14, F15, F16 |
| A2 | Testing "the phone" at 390 × 844 | 844 is the *screen*, not the viewport. The real Safari viewport is 664. A first screen that passes at 844 and fails at 664 passes nothing | F23, F24 |
| A3 | Treating 390 × 664 as the in-app row | It is Playwright's mobile-**Safari** viewport. Using it as the webview row tests Safari twice and the webview never | F24 |
| A4 | `user-scalable=no` or `maximum-scale=1` to stop webview zoom weirdness | Breaks low-vision reading and WCAG's 2× minimum; TikTok separately requires text "easy to read without having to zoom in", which is a design requirement, not a zoom-lock requirement | F7, F22 |
| A5 | Cleaning the URL on arrival ("strip `fbclid`, it's ugly") | Destroys `_fbc` construction and the dedup key; the ad platform then cannot see the conversion it paid for | F33, F34 |
| A6 | A door link built by hand that drops `utm_*` or `_gl` on the hop to the school site | GA4 starts a new session attributed to `gt.school / referral`; the landing gets blamed for traffic it delivered | F30 |
| A7 | `utm_source=x` for the X profile and X campaigns | `x` is absent from Google's source-category list, so `x / social_organic` lands in **Unassigned** and `x / paid_social` cannot reach **Paid Social** | F31, F32 |
| A8 | A consent banner that blocks tags from loading until the user interacts | Google's own documentation names this as a cause of data loss, because consent choices cannot then be verified | F41 |
| A9 | Sensationalised or withheld-information copy to raise CTR | Meta scores "withholding information, sensationalized language and engagement bait" as low-quality attributes against competing advertisers; it is also directly contrary to the register discipline in MD-16 and AD-11 | F9 |
| A10 | Any gate — email wall, quiz, "enter your details to continue" — before the page's substance | TikTok prohibits landing pages "that require viewers to download additional programs or enter personal information in order to access the main content" | F8 |
| A11 | Relying on a link-in-bio hop to a third-party tool | Instagram already allows five links, and the hop adds a webview navigation inside an environment that is already hostile to navigation; AD-12 already chose against it, and F43 supports the choice for a reason AD-12 does not state (the extra hop happens *inside* the in-app browser) | F43, F17 |
| A12 | Omitting `width=device-width` and trusting the webview | A bundled Android WebView does not use wide viewport mode by default (F21) and scales by target density (F21a), so the same markup lays out at a width neither Chrome nor the designer chose | F21, F21a |
| A13 | A door CTA whose hit area is the text node rather than the anchor | Already a known mirror hazard (`CLAUDE.md`: `button-text` + `button-text-ab` duplicate the accessible name), and in a webview with no back button a mis-tap is a lost session rather than a lost second | F17, F18 |
| A14 | Optimising creative to the visible Quality Score or quality-ranking number | Google states outright that Quality Score omits device, location, time of day and assets (F12a); Meta's ranking is relative to whoever else is bidding (F9). Both are diagnostics, not targets | F9, F12a |
| A15 | Launching the consent build at the same time as the baseline | Uplift reporting needs "at least 7 full days" of consent mode before it means anything (F44a), and REQ-027 already wants 28 days of Georgetown data before comparison | F44a, REQ-027 |

## 5. How it applies to GT

### 5a. The in-app viewport heights the QA matrix must include

`17` section 4 currently lists one class, "Phone in an app browser | 390 by 664, 412 by 780", with the note "Instagram and TikTok webviews take 150 to 180px". Two corrections follow from F24: **390 × 664 is Playwright's mobile-Safari viewport for `iPhone 13`/`iPhone 14`, not a webview**, and the "150 to 180px" figure describes Safari's chrome (measured 180 px on iPhone 13/14, 193 px on iPhone 15), not an app's. The matrix should therefore separate three tiers.

| Tier | Row | Basis | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mobile browser | **390 × 664** | Playwright `iPhone 13`/`iPhone 14` viewport; screen 390 × 844; chrome 180 px | **Measured** (F24) |
| Mobile browser | **393 × 659** | Playwright `iPhone 15`/`iPhone 15 Pro`; screen 393 × 852; chrome 193 px | **Measured** (F24) |
| Mobile browser | **430 × 739** | Playwright `iPhone 15 Pro Max`; screen 430 × 932; chrome 193 px | **Measured** (F24) |
| Mobile browser | **412 × 839** | Playwright `Pixel 7`; screen 412 × 915; chrome 76 px | **Measured** (F24) |
| Mobile browser | **393 × 727** | Playwright `Pixel 5`; screen 393 × 851; chrome 124 px | **Measured** (F24) |
| Mobile browser | **402 × 681** | iPhone 16 Pro / 17 logical 402 × 874 (F23) minus the 193 px chrome measured on the 393-wide models (F24). No Playwright descriptor exists | **Derived** — arithmetic shown |
| In-app, iOS | **390 × 600** and **393 × 596** | Safari small viewport (664 / 659) minus a **64 px** persistent bottom toolbar. 64 = a 44 CSS px minimum tappable control row (the runbook's own 44 × 44 rule, `17` section 4) plus a ~20 px bottom safe-area inset, which `viewport-fit=cover` obliges the page to respect (F22) | **Derived bound** — replaced by the REQ-024 measurement |
| In-app, iOS large | **430 × 675** | 739 − 64 | **Derived bound** |
| In-app, Android | **412 × 775** | 839 − 64 | **Derived bound** |

Three rules govern how those rows are used, and they matter more than the exact numbers.

1. **The in-app rows are bounds, not measurements, and they are labelled as such in `1_spec/13` until REQ-024 replaces them.** Neither Meta nor TikTok publishes a height (F13). The REQ-024 device pass must therefore *record* `window.innerHeight`, `screen.height`, `100svh`, `100lvh` and `100dvh` on each of Instagram iOS, Facebook iOS, TikTok iOS, Instagram Android and TikTok Android, and those numbers are written back into this file and into `17` section 4. A pass that only produces screenshots has not done the job.
2. **The in-app rows test `svh` behaviour, not just fit.** In a webview the toolbars do not retract (F15 against the definition in F14), so the page never receives the large viewport. The test asserts `100svh ≈ 100lvh ≈ 100dvh` in the webview; any mock whose first screen depends on the difference fails.
3. **The first-screen definition is unchanged but the target height is.** `17` section 4 defines "fits the screen" as header, umbrella sentence, provenance line and both door CTAs fully visible without scrolling. Against 596 px, not 844, that is the binding constraint on every mock. With 57% of viewing time above the fold and 74% in the first two screenfuls (F3), what does not fit in 596 px is not compensated for by what sits below it.

### 5c. The tag and parameter requirements the mocks carry as data attributes

The mocks already carry `data-event`, `data-school`, `data-placement`, `data-type`, `data-region-source` and `data-label` (125 / 81 / 57 / 36 / 9 occurrences across M1–M5), and REQ-029 keeps the mocks tracking-free. This cluster adds four requirements, all of which are markup the mocks can carry and the winner wires in S5.

| # | Requirement | Shape in the mock | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **`data-route` on the helper answers** | `data-event="helper_answer" data-route="georgetown\|anywhere"` | `1_spec/06` section 3 and `1_spec/03` section 7 both specify `helper_answer{route}` and no mock carries either attribute today. Without it the helper is unmeasurable and MD-15's "soft fit check" cannot be evaluated |
| R2 | **Drop `variant` from the mocks' attribute set, or carry it as a fixed `data-variant="1"`** | Today `1_spec/03` section 7 and `1_spec/06` section 3 attach `variant` to `door_click` and `compare_view`, but MD-03 was revised after R3 to **(c), one fixed headline at launch**. Carrying a variant parameter that no surface varies produces a dimension with one value | Resolve in the register, not in the markup |
| R3 | **Every outbound door and application link declares its parameter pass-through** | `data-event="door_click" data-school="…" data-passthrough="utm,gl,click_ids"` on the anchor, with the S5 wiring appending `school`, the incoming `utm_*`, `_gl`, and the click identifiers `fbclid`, `ttclid`, `gclid`, `_hsenc`, `_hsmi` | F30 (the `_gl` linker only survives a real link navigation), F33 (`_fbc` is built from `fbclid` on the destination), F35 (`ttclid`), F36/F43 (HubSpot identity). | R4 | **No `100vh` anywhere; `100svh` with a static fallback, and `viewport-fit=cover` paired with `env(safe-area-inset-*)` padding on anything fixed to an edge** | A checker rule over `0_plan/mockups/**/*.html`: fail on `100vh`, fail on `user-scalable=no`, fail on `maximum-scale` below 2 | F14, F15, F22 |

Two further requirements belong to the tracking plan rather than the markup, and both are corrections to what is written today:

- **`1_spec/06` section 9 must distinguish stripping from reporting.** It currently says `fbclid` and `_hsenc` "are left to GA4's default handling and excluded from landing-page reports", which is right, while AN-11 says "strip tracking parameters that could carry identifiers", which — if implemented as a redirect that rewrites the URL before the tags run — breaks `_fbc` (F33) and the 48-hour dedup window (F34). The rule to write: **exclude from reports and from the page-path dimension; never remove from the URL before the container has run.**
- **Consent Mode's mode is a design input.** Basic sends nothing before consent; Advanced sends cookieless pings and gets an advertiser-specific model (F42). AN-10 says the chosen mode "is recorded here" but does not yet record it. Whichever is chosen, the banner must not delay tag *loading* (F41).

| # | Question | Why it is open | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| OQ-1 | What are the *actual* viewport heights in the Instagram, Facebook and TikTok webviews on the devices GT's audience uses? | No vendor documents them (F13); every public discussion is qualitative (F15, F16). The 596 / 600 / 775 bounds in 5a are derived | Make REQ-024 record `innerHeight`, `screen.height`, `100svh`, `100lvh`, `100dvh` per app per OS, and write the measurements back into 5a. Until then the mocks are judged against the bound, which is the conservative direction |
| OQ-2 | Do the webviews retract their toolbars on scroll at all? | F14 defines `lvh`/`dvh` in terms of retractable UA chrome; F15 says webview toolbar behaviour is "non-standard" without saying what it is. The claim that `svh ≈ lvh ≈ dvh` in a webview is a deduction, not a measurement | Same device pass. If a webview *does* retract, the in-app rows relax; nothing in the design has to change, because designing for the small viewport is safe either way |
| OQ-3 | Does the container load fast enough inside a webview to read `fbclid` before the visitor leaves? | `1_spec/06` §2 loads the container "after first paint" for REQ-073; F17 reports webviews load slower. If the container has not run when the visitor bounces, the click is unattributed | Measure container-ready time in the REQ-024 pass.
