Two doors, two maps

v2 of the two-door panel. Same component, same measurements, with a map on each side: one pin in one state for the campus, the whole country filled for the online school.

The header, and the entrance

The white bar is gone. anywhere.gt.school carries its mark on the page's own ground rather than on a plate, and on a page whose subject is two photographs the plate was the loudest object. The mark is centred, which is also where the estate's own load animation starts it.

The entrance follows that animation: the mark begins in the middle of the screen and rises. Where the estate's then vanishes, this one lands in the header and stays, and the page fades in under it. It is CSS only, so no script is involved. Two notes against it, recorded rather than hidden: the studio's motion rules forbid animating the largest element on the first screen before first paint, and this is a replica of the estate's behaviour rather than a proposal for a landing page. Under reduced motion nothing moves and nothing fades, verified: no transform, full opacity, and zero running animations.

The markers

The circles become the GT mark, and the palette comes from the community map at apply.gt.school. That map is a live Mapbox map, so its artwork cannot be lifted, but its stylesheet was read: its most-used colour is the accent orange, with light gold, navy and a deeper navy behind them. The land here takes the light gold that both that map and the estate's static map use, and each marker is the GT mark alone, with no disc behind it.

One figure to weigh. The brand orange on the light-gold land measures 1.48 to 1. The marks are separated by a shadow rather than a plate, which is what keeps them visible at all, but they are deliberately faint. If they should read harder, the single change is the land: the same palette's navy would put the orange mark at 5.83 to 1 without touching anything else.

The marker positions are illustrative, not data. They are spread coast to coast to show reach. The estate's own static map does carry real per-state figures, but it was rejected for the reasons above and its geography stops before the east coast, so nothing here is derived from it. If these should sit on real locations, the community map holds them and someone with access should supply the list.

Why these maps, and not the estate's own

The estate has a map on anywhere.gt.school and it was tried first. It was rejected on two grounds, both found by looking rather than assuming.

It carries per-state student counts baked in as outlined paths, eleven of them, reading 732, 438, 121, 87, 66, 52, 42, 33, 25, 12 and 8. Those are figures, and this programme allows no figure on a new surface without a claims-register row. None of them has one, and their total sits oddly beside the register's own wording about the school's size.

And unclipping it showed why it could not carry this claim anyway: the artwork contains only the western two thirds of the country. There is no east coast in it at all. A map that stops at Michigan cannot be the picture for across the country.

The maps used instead are this programme's own, already built and reviewed in the direction called The Map: a Texas outline, a complete United States outline, and a pin placed at Georgetown. That direction also set the language this reuses, a pin for the campus and the filled country for the school, on the reasoning that the ink standing for reach should be a place rather than an empty outline.

Nothing here states a number. Georgetown shows one pin in one state. Anywhere shows the country filled and picks nothing out inside it, because highlighting one state on the panel whose whole claim is everywhere would argue the opposite.

Three faults only measurement would have caught

The font import did not resolve in this environment, so the monospace fell back to a wider face and the button came out 119px against the original's 104. The faces are now served from the page's own folder.

A flex item will not shrink below its own content width unless told to, which floored the unhovered panel.

And 24 pixels of panel padding, which I invented, silently floored the layout, because a zero flex basis still cannot go below a box's own padding. That alone was the difference between a 763/525 split and the correct 773/515, and it is why the left panel widened less than the right in an earlier published version. The original panel carries no padding at all.

What is copied from the live page, and is not a proposal

The photographs show identifiable children. Our own mocks use none, pending written permissions. The right-hand paragraph carries the learning-speed multiple, which our own decision keeps off any landing surface because the figure is unverified. Both are what the live page says today.

What was changed on purpose

Each panel carries its own school lockup, GT Georgetown on the left and GT Anywhere on the right, so the campus reads as GT Georgetown rather than only GT School. Each panel carries both a Learn more and an Apply now, where the original gives one action to each side. The estate has no precedent for two actions on a single panel, so the pairing follows its own header: an outlined secondary beside a filled primary. On a photograph the outline is white rather than the header's warm grey, and that is the only value on the page not taken from the source.

Both panels keep an identical construction, because ranking the two doors is the one thing this component must not do.

The original reveals on mouse hover only and cannot be reached from a keyboard. This responds to focus as well, and holds still under reduced motion.

The lockups, and where they came from

An earlier version set the school names as plain type under the GT School and GT Anywhere marks, which left the right panel repeating its own logo. Johnny supplied the brand sheet carrying the two sub-lockups, and they replace that type entirely, so the two doors are now exactly parallel.

Neither sub-lockup exists as an asset in this repository or on the live site, and both are drawn as outlined paths rather than editable type, so neither could be retyped. They were lifted from the brand sheet at full resolution and the flat navy dropped to transparency. The first attempt un-composited the colour as well and over-brightened the orange to 255,151,87, because the brand orange is not maximally distant from navy and dividing by that distance inflates it. The second keeps every colour as painted and derives transparency from distance alone: the ink now measures 229,140,84, within one of the brand value.

Still open: these are raster crops of a brand sheet, not vector artwork. They are sharp at the size used here, but a production build should take the real vector files from whoever holds the brand originals.
