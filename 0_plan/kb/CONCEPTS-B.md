### Getty's two-door pattern, precisely

- The shared sentence is exact and short: "Enjoy the art, architecture, and gardens of our two unique sites" (getty-1440-full.jpg, under "Our Locations") — twelve words, plain, no adjective ranking either site.
- The two links read as a described pair, not a card-with-photo-and-button: "Visit the Getty Villa Museum" and "Visit the Getty Center," stacked, same weight, same underline treatment, inside one card that shares a single generic museum-interior photograph rather than one photo per site — a different photo strategy than GT will likely need, since K9 §10.5 pushes GT toward a deliberate per-door imagery decision, not a shared illustration.
- The compare control is real (per K0's markup fetch) but is not part of the visible page in any of the four captures — it lives inside the "Visit ▾" dropdown, one hover-interaction away from the doors it is supposed to sit beside. A parent scrolling on a phone (no hover) may never discover it exists.
- The footer repeats the two-site parity at the most mundane level possible — complete addresses and independently-formatted hours for both sites, side by side, same column width (getty-1440-full.jpg, footer) — arguably a stronger proof of "these are equals" than the hero card, because it is unglamorous and therefore uncontrived.
- Net finding for GT: Getty is the right shape (shared sentence, two equal links, a compare idea) but the wrong timing and the wrong depth for the compare control. GT's version must compress Getty's two screens into one and must put "compare" on the page, not in a menu.
