### Where the two vocabularies conflict

| Conflict | Research-institution vocabulary | Elite-prestige vocabulary | Evidence | Consequence for GT |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Color and warmth | Restraint: one accent against black/white/gray (RC-01) | Also restrained, but paired with warm photography and plain-language headlines (RC-09) | S1, S4, S8 vs S12, S14, S15 | No real conflict here; both vocabularies favor restraint. DD-02's kept palette is compatible with either |
| Proof as hero vs. story as hero | Data-forward: mono captions, grids, count-ups are native to MIT's identity-system layer (RC-02, RC-03) | None of the seven K-12/gifted sites fetched (S12, S14-S16, S18, S20, S21) lead their homepage with a statistic; all lead with a mission sentence or a photo | S6, S7 vs S12, S14-S16, S18, S20, S21 | This is the sharpest real conflict. GT's own brief (DD-09) asks for both at once. The resolution the evidence supports: keep the mono/grid/data treatment inside a clearly bounded proof module (as M2 already scopes it), never as the first-screen hero, matching what every K-12 comparable actually does even when their underlying selectivity is extreme |
| Anti-marketing vs. produced prestige | MIT's admissions credibility comes from looking unproduced (RC-06) | Every elite K-12 site fetched is fully produced institutional marketing (professional photography, edited copy, no raw student blogging on the homepage) | S5 vs S12, S14-S16, S18, S20, S21 | GT cannot do both. A paid-social landing page (MO-5) has to be produced, which puts it firmly in the elite-K-12 register, not MIT's blog register |
| Stating the selectivity number | Not observed at MIT (RC-07); no acceptance-rate claim found on the admissions homepage | Mixed: most schools imply selectivity through process (Lakeside, Nueva, Harker say nothing numeric); Davidson Academy Online states "99.9th percentile" outright (RC-13) | S5 vs S12, S14, S15, S20 | DD-10's "state the 90th-percentile bar" sits with the gifted-testing convention (Davidson), against the broader prestige-school convention (Lakeside/Nueva/Harker) and against MIT. That is a defensible, deliberate choice given GT's category, and worth stating as such rather than as a universal "elite schools do this" claim |
| A third vocabulary neither lens named | N/A | N/A | RC-17, RC-18, RC-21 (S26, S23, S28) | The plan's L3-vs-L4 framing (`05` section 3) treats the conflict as MIT-technical versus prep-school-heritage. In practice GT inherits a third, unnamed vocabulary by DNA: the network's own consumer-tech/hype register (superlative claims, thin footnotes, "crush"/"unlock" verbs). DD-09's "no cold register," DD-10's "no wealth cues" and Nari's substance complaint (00:08:49, cited in DD-16/MN-08) already reject this third vocabulary in effect; it is worth naming explicitly so it isn't reintroduced by accident through inherited copy or imagery |
