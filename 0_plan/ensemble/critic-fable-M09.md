## The four rulings

**1. Withdrawing the reveal entirely, rather than keeping it over a visible rest state.** Right, and better than what
I asked for. My fix said the line must show at rest and allowed the hover treatment to stay; the engineer read the
next step, which is that a reveal over an already-visible line is not a reveal — it is a 200ms opacity change on
text the reader is already reading, triggered by an input two thirds of sessions do not have (DD-14), and K4-P12
exists precisely to stop that. Keeping it would also have kept a whole-room hover trigger over what is now a
whole-room link, which is the mismatch that cost the Objective and Motion rows at iteration 0. What it costs the
direction is small and worth naming: M09 will be the one mock in the review that never moves, so in a side-by-side
with directions that do, it can read as the static option — a presentation risk, not a design defect, and the
answer is to show Nari the hover underline and the focus ring, which are real states. The programme cost is
sharper: M09 was the studio's best case for testing whether K4 §8.1 V2 is worth having at all, and that evidence is
now a paragraph in a rationale rather than a built artefact. Nothing is missing at the interaction level: hover
underlines the room's own label, focus draws K3's ring, and the door state is V1, which the contract offers for this
exact component.

**2. Refusing fix 2 and moving the slack below the mark.** Upheld — and I tested it rather than taking the argument.
Two readings of "cap" exist and both are worse than what was built. Capping only the rooms (`.places { flex: 0 0
auto }`, which is what I asked for) ends the rooms at **783** at both 1440 and 1920 and leaves **53 and 113px of
bare cream between the rooms' bottom edge and the floor rule that is supposed to be their floor**: the pair stops
reading as two rooms running to the fold and starts reading as two cards floating above a line — my capture
`crit2-capped-1920.png` is the whole argument, and colour-to-the-fold is this direction's thesis. Capping the first
screen as well (`min-height: auto`) pulls everything up and leaves the last ink **54px clear of the 900 edge at
1440 and 114px clear of 960 at 1920** (`cf2.mjs`), which is the false floor the runner forbids. So the engineer's
conclusion is right. Its stated reason is not quite: the quoted "25 pixels clear of the 900 edge" reproduces under
neither reading, and under the weaker cap the compare sentence still lands 1px inside the edge, so the ink test
alone does not decide it. The real reason is the one in my capture — a capped room detaches from its own floor and
abandons the colour edge before the fold. Correct the stated reason; keep the build.

**3. Introducing subgrid.** Accepted, and it is the rare case where a new mechanism is the only honest one. I
defeated it and measured the two alternatives myself (`cf3.mjs`, `critic-m09-counterfactual.mjs`). With a fixed gap
under the line and no shared rows, the two door lines wrap to different heights across the middle of the range and
the pair is ranked by the layout: the CTAs and the marks part by **25, 26, 26, 26 and 26 pixels at 800, 1023, 1080,
1150 and 1200**, and at 768 the buttons differ by 9px in height (53 against 44) — exactly the defect Astra named.
With an auto margin instead, the bottoms align but every spare pixel goes into the gap above the button, which is
the void this iteration was convened to close (measured: CTA bottoms fall to 699 at 1440 and 759 at 1920). Subgrid
is the only one of the three that satisfies DD-26 and C01 — the two doors equal by construction — at every width
without re-opening the hole. The degradation story is accurate: where `grid-template-rows: subgrid` is unsupported
the declaration drops and each room stacks the same five parts in the same order, losing the shared alignment and
