## The thesis question, answered on the picture

The brief's thesis is that GT School is one programme with Guides in both schools and the days differ only in where
the child sits. **The two timelines illustrate two schedules side by side. They do not argue that.** Four things,
each measured rather than read.

**1. Nothing is drawn between the two columns.** The claim is an equality, and an equality needs a shared object.
What the band ships is two closed panels, each with its own outline, its own tint and its own rail, separated by a
32 to 64px gutter of bare cream with nothing crossing it (my crop `dayband-1280.png`; `V14-full.jpg`, the day
band). The equality exists only as an alignment of two 9px squares **574px apart** at 1440. There is no shared
floor rule, no shared axis, no bracket, no caption. The band heading "A day at GT" carries the one-programme claim
in words; the drawing adds nothing to it that the reader can see.

**2. The rail's only spatial variable is copy length, not time.** My probe, node centre as a fraction of the rail
from first node to finish mark: Georgetown's "Afternoon" sits at **54.1%** at 1440 and at 1280, **63.9%** at 390,
**64.1%** at 768. GT Anywhere's "Across the year" sits at **68.4%** at 1440 and **64.0%** at 390. The same
afternoon moves ten points down the same day when the phone narrows, and at 768 and 390 the two rails are
structurally identical to a tenth of a percent — the "two days are not the same length or the same rhythm"
the rationale claims (Direction, line 37) is a 1440-only artefact of where two sentences happen to wrap. A parent
who reads the rail as a day is reading text-fitting as chronology.

**3. The two sequences do not share an axis, and parallel rails say they do.** Georgetown's stops are times of day;
GT Anywhere's are a day and a year. Drawn as parallel rails with aligned terminals, "ACROSS THE YEAR" is placed
opposite "AFTERNOON" and the two are offered as corresponding moments (`dayband-1280.png`). The rationale names
the asymmetry honestly in prose and then draws it away. This is M10's fault in a different geometry: the picture
argues sameness of shape, which the evidence does not support, while the argument the evidence *does* support —
same school, same bar, same Guides — is carried by the table's repeated cells and the proof strip, not by the
drawing.

**4. Below 768 the object is not there at all.** My probe, finish-mark bottoms: V1 **1338.5 / 1657.5**, V3
**1313.3 / 1633** — 319 and 320px apart. The panels stack, so "one finish" has no referent, and the two bodies are
not even equal in height at **V6 (263.9 against 239.8)** and **V10 (195.1 against 220.2)**, which the rationale
does not report because it measured 1440 only. On the eleven phone rows — two thirds of sessions, DD-14 — the band
is two lists with an unexplained orange square at the foot of each (`day-390.png`, my capture). That square is
`aria-hidden` and is never named in words anywhere on the page, so its meaning is available to nobody.

**What would make it argue.** One drawn element that belongs to both columns: a single floor rule in
`--color-border-strong` running the full band width under both panels with the two terminals sitting on it, named
once in words from `copy.md` section 9, and kept on the phone by drawing it across the stacked pair or by
captioning it. Then the equality is an object a reader can see rather than a coincidence of two bottom edges, and
it survives stacking and forced colours.

## The four rulings

**1. Door parity at the 320 reflow row.** The arithmetic is right and I reproduced it: both cards 272px wide, the
button box **254px**, the Georgetown label 246.5px, and the label wraps to two lines so the button stands
**52.5 against 44** (my probe; `v18-doors.png`, my crop). The conclusion that the label cannot be made to fit is
correct. The conclusion that nothing can be done is not, because **the requirement is not that the label fits —
it is that the two doors carry equal weight (DD-26)**. Equalising is one declaration: under `max-width: 359px`,
give `.door-cta` a two-line `min-height` so both buttons stand 52.5. Nothing is removed, no padding goes to zero,
the arrow stays, the cards stay equal, and V18 still has no horizontal scroll. **Accepted as low-impact** — 320 is
the floor of the matrix and V18 is the WCAG 1.4.10 reflow row — **but the stated reason is wrong and the cheap fix
should be taken.**

**2. The GT icon off the doors.** **Accepted.** `gt-icon.svg` is entirely `#E48B53`; the brief assigns this
screen's single accent use to the compare anchor's rule; two 32px orange glyphs beside the door labels would be
the largest orange objects on the screen even under M10's ruling that a logo's own colour does not spend the
`--color-accent` budget. M15 made the same call on the same reasoning. What I do **not** accept is the sentence
attached to it: DD-04(a), as amended, asks for "explicit format and location labels with supporting icons", and a
9px filled square is a bullet, not an icon — it carries no meaning until a reader has learned it, and the page
never teaches it. The redundancy DD-04 wants is met here by **label, name and tint**, which is enough for REQ-012's
ten seconds; the rationale should say that rather than claiming the node fills the icon slot. The node's real cost
is separate and is ruling 4's neighbour: it is the key the timelines read, and it is asymmetric.

**3. The reveal not built.** **Accepted without reservation, and it is the right reading of the brief.** D-039 is
Escalated; the contract is explicit that a mock ships its no-JavaScript state until it decides; and the brief's own
Acceptance row requires four steps visible with no scroll trigger. Those two requirements are in tension with the
Motion row's V6, and shipping still is the only resolution that satisfies both. The avoided concept the brief names
is C33 — AREA 17's regions blank at capture — and it is avoided completely: every step is in the markup and painted
on first paint at all nineteen rows, in normal and reduced-motion rendering alike. The engineer's second reason is
the better one and should be kept in the rationale: a reveal over content that must be complete without it is not a
reveal. Declining V8 for the same direction is also right, and its stated ground — the rail is "the least
informative thing on the screen" — is the mock's own correct diagnosis of the fault in fixes 1 to 3.

**4. The connectors at 1.42 to 1.56 against the tints.** **Not accepted.** I recomputed from `tokens-v2.css`:
`--color-border` is **1.56 on `--color-bg`, 1.42 on the Georgetown tint, 1.44 on the Anywhere tint** — the
contract's figures exactly. **The rule postdates this build and that must be said plainly: the brief's own Colour
row specifies `--color-border` for the connectors, and the engineer followed its brief and flagged the consequence
rather than changing it silently. That is the right conduct.** But the rule is not new reasoning; it is M10's fix
written down, and the defence offered here — "reinforcement, not the thing that has to be seen" (RATIONALE.md:215)
— is verbatim the defence the contract records three mocks making and being wrong about, because it "is true of a
decorated page and false of a drawn one". This direction's hero is a drawing. The rail and the ticks are its only
continuous elements. And the specific object that fails hardest is not a decoration by anyone's account: the
panel's **floor**, which the two terminals straddle and which is the datum for the whole "same finish" claim, is
the 1.42 hairline while the panel's ceiling is `--color-text` at 16.10. The finish mark itself is worse —
`--color-accent` at 2.17, 2.19 and 2.38, which K12 F88 and the contract both name as a failure — and it is the one
object the rationale calls "the band's argument". `--color-border-strong` is already in the token file and already
on this page's door edges; it is a three-line change. Take it, and the brief's Colour row should be amended with
it.

## Verdict

**Iterate.** Objective 5, Composition 3, Typography 5, Imagery 3, Colour and surface 3, Motion 5, Mission and
exclusivity 4, Craft 4, Continuity 4 — three rows below 4: **Composition**, because the direction's own object does
not exist below 768 and is not shown at N; **Imagery**, because the GT Anywhere mark loses its own name in forced
colours; and **Colour and surface**, because the diagram that carries the argument is drawn in a decoration token
and terminated in an accent that fails on every ground it touches. The first screen, the type, the stillness and
the copy discipline are gate-ready today; the band is not, and the band is the direction.
