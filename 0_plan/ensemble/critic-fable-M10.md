## The seven rulings asked for

**1. Doors first below 1180 rather than the brief's 1024. Upheld, and the number reproduces exactly.** I did the arithmetic independently: at 1024 the container is the viewport, `--space-6` takes 32px a side leaving 960, twelve tracks with eleven 32px gutters give 50.67 per track, so columns one to seven are 7 × 50.67 + 6 × 32 = **546.7px**. The landscape viewBox is 700 wide, so at 547 the transform scale is 0.781 and the labels would render at 14.62 × 0.781 = **11.4px**. He refused that and was right to; `V13.jpg` shows the stacked form working, with the doors at 411 of 744 and the map teased below the fold. The ruling carries a sting, and it is the sting that structures this whole card: **he refused 11.4px labels at 1024 and ships 11.07px at V18 and 12.70px at V1 without knowing it**, because the harness measured the declared size rather than the rendered one. The reasoning that justifies this departure is the same reasoning that condemns the shipped labels. Accept the breakpoint; apply its logic to the rows it was not applied to.

**2. The map capped at 700 CSS px rather than scaling with its column. Upheld in principle, rejected as executed.** Capping the rendered width is the only way to hold SVG text at a fixed size, and where it is done properly it works perfectly: my probe reports a screen-CTM scale of **exactly 1.0000** at V11, V12, V13, V15, V16, V17 and V19 — every wide row where the map reaches its cap — because the cap (700) equals the viewBox (700). The portrait composition does not do this. Its cap is `24rem` = 384 against a 344-unit viewBox, so it is designed to a scale of 1.116 and delivers anything from 0.79 to 1.11. The cap is not the problem; the mismatch is, and the uncapped rows are. Two costs the rationale does not price. It surrenders the brief's "V16 map 460 tall" for a 290px map that is **89px narrower than its own grid track** at 1440 and 1920, which is what gives the module three right edges (fix 6). And it buys, in exchange, a type guarantee it does not actually deliver. If fix 2 lands, this departure becomes unnecessary: with the labels in HTML the map can scale with its column freely, which is what the brief asked for in the first place.

**3. No school mark on the door cards. Rejected as argued, and the fix is free.** His arithmetic is exact and I reproduced it: a 20px horizontal mark inline with the label, plus the navy plate the GT Anywhere wordmark needs, moves the second CTA from 590.2 to **612.2 at V2**, over the 605 limit by **7.2px** against **14.8px** of slack. Every number he gives is right, including "22px per pair". But he costed the expensive option. DD-04's cue set reads "Label plus icon for each door (**`gt-icon.svg`** or the school mark)", and `gt-icon.svg` is the neutral GT mark, which needs no navy plate on either card — the plate rule at CONTRACT.md section 1 governs the white-lettering *wordmark*, not the icon. M01, the gated mock, does exactly this: `gt-icon.svg` at 32px in a left column spanning the label and name rows (`M01/styles.css:106`), the same mark on both doors, one school and two ways in. I built that variant of M10 and measured it at the same rows: **the CTA bottoms do not move** — 590.2 at V2, 619.9 at V1, 576.2 at V3, 547.8 at V5, 294.6 at V10, 411.5 at V13, identical to the shipped page — because a 32px icon spanning the label and name rows fits inside 54px that already exist. The cue set is available at zero fold cost. This is the one departure in the file whose premise is sound and whose conclusion is wrong, and it matters beyond the icon: the cue set is a redundancy requirement from REQ-012's ten-second test, and this page currently carries the choice on label, name and tint alone — which in forced colours reduces to label and name, since `forced-colors.jpg` flattens both tints to Canvas.

**4. The land outline takes `--color-text` rather than the brief's `--color-border`. Upheld without reservation, and it is the best judgement call in the file.** I measured the alternatives rather than accepting the reason: `--color-border` #cac6c4 is **1.42:1** against the land fill and **1.56:1** against the page ground — at phone scale the state would have no edge at all, which is not a stylistic loss but the loss of the shape the direction is built on. `--color-border-strong` at **3.63:1** passes 1.4.11 but is the wrong instrument for a 1px cartographic line, which is what he found in the capture. `--color-text` at 1px is correct and the inset stays properly subordinate at `--color-text-muted` (6.25:1). The departure is declared, measured and right. It is also the precedent that decides the next ruling.

**5. Ring stroke weights grow as their opacity falls. Rejected.** The diagnosis is right and the remedy is wrong. His three numbers reproduce on my measurement — **1.87, 1.49 and 1.21:1** over the cream ground, 1.85, 1.48 and 1.21 where the rings cross the land fill — so he knew exactly how faint they were, and chose to keep "the brief's colour spec exactly" and compensate with weight. Three reasons that fails. **Weight is not contrast**: WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1 is a luminance ratio, and thickening an invisible line makes a wider invisible line, which is precisely what `crop-map-1440.jpg` shows for ring 3. **The rings are in scope**: they are parts of a graphic required to understand the content — the label "online from anywhere" points at them and at nothing else — so the exception for essential presentation does not apply, because nothing about the information requires *these* alphas. **And the precedent is his own**: one departure earlier he overrode the brief's `--color-border` because 1.4:1 left the land with no edge, then preserved the brief's alphas at 1.21:1 for the rings. A brief is not a decision row, and CONTRACT.md section 4 does not authorise sub-3:1 for a graphic that carries meaning. The fix is already written in this stylesheet and only runs under `forced-colors: active`; promote it (fix 1). The strongest argument for doing so is the pair of captures themselves: in `forced-colors.jpg` the three rings are solid, dashed and dotted at one weight and they read as three expanding orders of reach; in `V14.jpg` they read as one circle and two smudges.

**6. The Locations list shows Georgetown alone. Upheld without reservation, and it is the most disciplined decision in the file.** gt.school's own footer publishes Miami FL, New York NY and Bay Area CA as "Coming soon" (K12 F64); K12 **OQ-3** — "Is the four-location footer list approved and current?" — is open and addressed to Nari (I read the row); `copy.md` section 9 permits those cities only with her confirmation recorded in a rationale, and no such confirmation exists. He withheld all three. The cost is not rhetorical. The Locations list is this direction's *declared* N-campus proof and it is one row; the map shows one pin; and fact 4 of the operating model — "expanding nationwide" — is therefore the one thing this page cannot show. A weaker build would have shown them and cited gt.school as the source. He also built for the confirmation instead of arguing for it: adding a city is one `<li>` and one `<circle>`, the doors and the list both run on auto-fit tracks, and he fixed the live component's three defects on the way — a real link rather than the `href="#"` K12 A13 records, no `vh`, and the status in `--color-text` on `--color-surface` rather than the gold of K12 F65, which fails as text. Nothing to change. When OQ-3 closes, the map gains three pins and the thesis gains its best evidence.

**7. The section reveal is not built because D-039 is escalated. Upheld, and forced.** D-039 hit its three-attempt ceiling on 2026-09-11 (`12-register-loop.md:107` and row 10 of the escalation log), so no script is available to any mock, and the contract's section 6 says a mock ships its no-JavaScript state until it is decided. V6 is a third-screen-and-later move that changes nothing about the argument; the page is complete at rest and the runner confirms every duration 0s. There is nothing here to fix and nothing the engineer could have done differently. One observation that is not a fault and should survive into the spec: the brief also rules out a map draw, because no map move exists in K4 section 8.1 and a draw would need a D-031 proposal. He could have reached for V8's diagram draw by analogy and did not. That restraint is correct twice over — once procedurally, and once for this direction specifically, because an animated expanding ring is exactly the broadcast-radius reading the drawing already invites and must not reinforce.

## Does the map earn its place?

**At 1180 and above, yes — conditionally. Below it, the direction does not exist. And as drawn, the map argues the wrong half of its own thesis.** Three findings, each measured.

**It is absent where the readers are.** Two thirds of sessions are mobile. My probe puts the map's top edge at **0.97 to 1.38 viewport heights** down the page on every phone row, and at **exactly 1.00** at V9 (390 × 844), the reference phone — so a parent must scroll one full screen to meet it. What they see instead (`V9.jpg`) is a header, the sentence, and two door cards: a first screen indistinguishable from M01's. The direction named "The Map" shows no map to most of its readers on the screen where the choice is made. That is not a fault of the reorder, which is right, and not a reason to force the map up — it is a reason to say plainly that this is a laptop direction with a competent phone fallback, and to judge the map on what it does at 1280 and 1440.

**There, it argues Georgetown.** Count the ink in `crop-map-1440.jpg`: a pin, a horizontal leader, the label "Georgetown, Texas", a filled state, and an inset whose entire function is to say where Texas is — five devices, all of them locating the campus. For GT Anywhere: three rings at 1.87, 1.49 and 1.21:1 and a bullseye key glyph. And the rings are **closed**. I tested the outermost ring against each viewBox at every row: contained on all four edges at V1, V2, V3, V5 and V18 — every phone row — and clipped at the bottom only on the wide ones. `crop-map-390.jpg` is the clearest statement of the problem: a bounded circle centred on Georgetown that does not reach the state's own edges, under a caption reading "online from anywhere". A closed circle around a campus is the cartographic idiom for catchment, drive time or broadcast range. It is the idiom for *reach from here*, which is the exact opposite of the claim. Meanwhile the country is drawn and left empty. A parent in Miami — one of the cities gt.school itself publishes — looks at that and sees a school whose world stops somewhere north of San Antonio. (The rationale's own Direction paragraph says the rings go "off the frame"; measured, they do not on any phone row.)

**So today it is an illustration standing where an argument should be — and it is one move from being the argument.** The move is cheap and needs no new geometry, because `#m10-us` and `#m10-tx-in-us` are already fitted with a single transform. Invert the fill logic: fill the **country** as the GT Anywhere surface with the state picked out inside it, instead of filling the state and leaving the country an empty outline. Let the rings leave the frame on every edge in both compositions. Then the two halves of the sentence each have a shape — a pin for the campus, a filled continent for the school — and the diagram stops being a locator and starts being a claim.

I would not cut the map. It is the only distinctive object on the page — below the fold this is four cream bands and a table (`V14-full.jpg`) — its geometry is honest to a standard I verified end to end from primary sources, its forced-colours behaviour is the best in the slate, and it answers PQ-04 better than any sentence could. But a map is the most decorative thing any mock in this slate does, and a decorative object on this page has to pay for itself by arguing something the copy cannot. Right now it argues the half the copy already covers, at the widths where a parent has the most room to read, on a page whose single job is not to favour Georgetown. Fix 3 is the difference between a picture of where the campus is and a picture of what the school is.
