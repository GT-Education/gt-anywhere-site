## The two checker-forbidden items: my ruling

**The specimen number. The checkers are right to stop it, and wrong as a reason to drop it — but the page is better
without it anyway, for a reason the rationale does not give.**

R5 is not a prohibition on digits; it is a hand-curated whitelist. `check-mocks.py:8` already admits `'512'`, `'270'`,
`'9481'` (a phone number), `'2026'`, `'09'`, `'10'` (a date) and — decisively — **`'390'`, `'844'` and `'1440'`**, three
viewport figures that make no claim about the school and that no current mock even prints (I checked every
`mockups/M*/index.html` through the checker's own `visible_text`). So the rule as written already embodies "no figure
the studio has not decided on", not "no number that makes no claim", and the engineer's framing — that both checkers
"read 21 as a figure with no register row" — describes the behaviour correctly while mis-stating what the rule is for.
`check-claims.py` is even clearer: it maps figures to register rows because a figure on a school's page asserts
something about the school, and "M21" asserts nothing. The correct move was therefore the one the Departures table
itself names — propose the whitelist entry and let Johnny decide — and the engineer did raise it in two places rather
than smuggling it, which is the behaviour the register loop exists to produce.

Two things follow. First, if it is whitelisted, whitelist the *pattern*, not the token: adding a bare `'21'` would let
"21" appear anywhere on any page as an unsourced claim, whereas stripping `M\d\d` before the digit scan admits a
specimen number keyed to the mock id and nothing else. Second, and this is the part neither the brief nor the rationale
says: **a specimen number is a mock-only device.** On the artefact Nari reviews it is the signature of the language and
its absence costs the direction a visible piece of its argument — the sheet is marked but not identified, and the one
device that says "this is a drawing of a page" is missing. On a page a parent actually loads, "SPECIMEN M21" is an
internal id leaking onto a public surface with no meaning to the reader. So: whitelist the pattern, put the number on
the mock so the reviewer sees the whole language, and record that it does not ship. The wordless registration cross is
the right shipping form and the engineer found it unaided.

**The fold position beside each door. The checkers are right, the brief is wrong, and the engineer's reason is the
second-best one available.**

The rationale rejects it as "a digit", which is the same R5 argument. The stronger objection is that the figure cannot
be *true*. A fold position is a property of the viewport, not of the page: it is 587 at V2, 301 at V10, 501 at V16 and
a different number at each of the nineteen rows. A static mock with no JavaScript — which is what D-039 obliges this
mock to be — can only hard-code one of them, so the annotation would be wrong at eighteen rows out of nineteen, on a

## The Literata font-box claim: verified, and it belongs in the contract

**True, with one correction to how it is described, and the clearance figures do not rest on nothing.**

I checked the font binary rather than the page. `Literata-Regular.ttf`: `unitsPerEm` 1000, `hhea` ascender **1177**,
descender **−308**, and `OS/2` version 4 with `fsSelection` bit 7 (`USE_TYPO_METRICS`) **set**, `sTypoAscender` /
`sTypoDescender` 1177 / −308 and `usWinAscent` / `usWinDescent` 1177 / 308. All three metric sets agree, so the content
box is **1.485 em on every platform**, not just this one. Against a `--leading-tight` 1.15 line box the half-leading is
negative and the font box stands **0.1675 em proud on each side**: 7.71px at 46px, 5.70px at 34px, 4.69px at 28px. The
rationale's "0.17 to 0.18 em … 8px at the 46px h1, 6px at the 34px h2, 5px at the 28px door name" is right at all three
sizes, and the browser agrees — my probe reads the h1's `Range` client rects at **68px tall against a 52.9px line box
at V16** (canvas `fontBoundingBoxAscent` 54 + `fontBoundingBoxDescent` 14 = 68), so 7.55px per side after Chrome's
rounding. The `0.2em` spread at `:112` is 9.2px at 46px, 6.8px at 34px: a correct floor with about 1.5px to spare.

One correction, which is why it is worth writing down properly rather than copying the sentence across five briefs.
What overflows is the **font box**, not the ascender. My probe's `actualBoundingBox` for "GT School gifted campus" at
46px is 35.97 up and 10.58 down against a baseline sitting 46.45px into the line box, so ascender ink stops **10.5px
inside** the block's top edge, while descender ink on a block's last line clears its bottom edge by **4.1px**. So the
real ink that a grid line could show through is a `p` or a `g` on a last line, not an ascender on a first line — and
separately, the harness's own occlusion test compares occluders against *font-box* run rects (`M21-measure.mjs`, the
`o.t <= run.t + 1 && o.b >= run.b - 1` test), which is why the spread has to reach the full 0.1675 em rather than the
0.09 em the ink needs. Both paths lead to the same floor, so the 0 crossings and the 7.80px minimum stand.

**It generalises, and it should be lifted.** I parsed the other two faces for comparison: `Inconsolata-SemiBold` is
1.049 em and `InterTight-Regular` is 1.210 em, both smaller than any leading the contract uses, so neither ever
overflows. **Literata is the only one of the three faces whose content box exceeds its line box, and it does so at
`--leading-tight` 1.15 and at `--leading-body` 1.25 alike** — which means every direction in the programme that sets a
Literata heading over a drawn or tinted background has the same 0.1675 em of invisible overhang, and only this one has
measured it. Recommend a row in CONTRACT.md section 3: *Literata's content box is 1.485 em (ascent 1177, descent 308,
`USE_TYPO_METRICS` set); at `--leading-tight` it stands 0.1675 em proud of its line box on each side, so any knockout,
plate or clip behind a Literata heading needs at least 0.17 em of spread.*
